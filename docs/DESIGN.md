# Game design

## Pitch

A roguelike chemical-engineering game styled as a dark DCS/HMI control-room screen (black background, shaded gray vessels, cyan tags, black value boxes with green numbers, softkey bar along the bottom). Each run rolls its own chemistry and deals you random equipment. You build a flowsheet (reactors, separation columns, splitters, mixers, pumps, tanks) that turns a fixed feed into a product spec, tune it live until it makes on-spec product, then extend that same plant contract after contract.

## Decided

- 2D, Python, pygame-ce.
- **Build, then run and tune it yourself.** Drag equipment from the inventory (each card has an icon), click an out port then an in port to pipe it. RUN starts the plant live; everything stays editable. There is no pass/fail screen after a run: you watch the colors and readouts and figure out what is wrong.
- **Removing units.** Any placed unit (not FEED/PRODUCT/WASTE) can go back to the inventory at any time: right-click, Delete/Backspace on the selected unit, the faceplate TO INV key, or drag to the panel. Its pipes are removed and its contents lost; its settings (setpoint, split) are kept.
- **Undo/redo.** Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z), plus softkeys, for layout edits only: placing, moving, piping, removing pipes, sending units back. Up to 100 steps, cleared when a new contract starts. Process state is not rewound: a unit brought back by undo comes back empty. Pump setpoints and splitter ratios are not undo steps.
- **Tidy.** T or the TIDY softkey auto-arranges the plant into left-to-right columns following the flow (FEED, PRODUCT and WASTE stay put), lines up units in series so their pipes run straight, and parks unpiped units along the top. It is one undo step, so Ctrl+Z puts the hand layout back.
- **Tuning.** Every pump has a dealt flow range (e.g. 30-150 GPM) with a tunable setpoint; splitters tune 0-100% in 5% steps. Faceplate panel (click a unit), mouse wheel over a unit, or arrow keys.
- **Rate matters.** Reactors and columns have a rated GPM. Above it, reactor conversion drops and column splits smear toward 50/50 (load box turns red). Faster finishes sooner but risks going off-spec, so there is a sweet spot to find.
- **Contracts.** A contract is 1-3 orders: deliver ≥ N gal of a product at ≥ P% purity, each into its own PRODUCT outlet (PRODUCT, PRODUCT 2, PRODUCT 3; outlets are added as contracts grow and never taken away). The main order is the level's new product at the level's purity; extra orders ask for earlier products at their own purity (60-90%, looser or stricter than the main one) and 30-60% of its size. Contract 1 has one order, contract 2 one or two, contract 3 two, later ones two or three, and never fewer than the outlets already built. Each arriving stream is graded at its outlet: only streams at the outlet's purity count; off-spec streams are rejected (shown in the header). The contract completes the moment every order is met. Thresholds, not optimization, so many designs pass.
- **Running out.** Feed is finite (15,000 gal + 1,000 per level). Running dry before finishing offers a restart (restock feed, empty the plant, keep layout and settings); 2 restarts per contract, then the run is over. Restart is also a softkey.
- **Display.** Starts fullscreen; F11 toggles, Ctrl+Q quits. The game draws at a fixed 1280x720 and SDL scales it (letterboxed on non-16:9 screens), so the canvas does not grow with the screen.
- **Pipe readability.** Pipes route orthogonally; parallel pipes that would overlap are pushed into separate lanes, and where a horizontal run crosses another pipe's vertical run it draws a line jump (arc) over it. Hovering a pipe highlights it and shows its flow and composition (% and GPM per species).
- **Color.** One color per letter, in order: A blue, B green, C orange, D magenta, E cyan..., waste W always brown. The legend marks the waste. Pipes, tank levels and reactor sight glasses show the volume-weighted blend of what they carry, so a stream turns toward its species' color as it gets purer. Moving dots show flow.
- **Persistence.** The plant (placed equipment, pipes, inventory) and money carry over to the next contract. Later contracts build on what you made: the next target is made from species your plant already produces.
- **Random chemistry per run (procedural).** Letters are fixed so they are never confusing: the feed is A (60%) or A+B mixed 50/50-70/30 (40%), products take the next letters in the order they are unlocked, waste is always W. What is random is the stoichiometry. The run's 4-step chain is rolled once and stays the same all run, unlocking one reaction per level: reaction 1 makes the first product from the feed; every later reaction uses at least one product the plant already makes (newer ones likelier), maybe with one more species it has, with coefficients 1-3 (e.g. A + 2B → C, then A + 2C → D, then 2B + C → E). Each reaction uses a different set of species. All orders of a contract together are capped at 35% of the theoretical max yield so any roll stays achievable. Past the end of the chain contracts ask for more volume, higher purity, and a feed contaminated with waste.
- **Random equipment.** Reactors (conversion 45–90%, rated 80–250 GPM, 35% chance of a side reaction that degrades a species into waste), columns (random per-species split to the top outlet: some species sharp to top, the rest sharp to bottom, 2–15% leak; rated 100–300 GPM), pumps (max 150–600 GPM, min 10–30% of max), tanks (2k–20k gal), splitters, mixers.
- **No dead runs.** The starting hand and each new-reaction grant include a high-purity column (1–5% leak) that sends that reaction's product one way and its reactants the other. Two-feed runs also start with an extra random column.
- **Money.** Completing a contract pays base ($600 + $300 per level) (× 1.35 for each order beyond the first) plus a purity bonus: base × 1.5 × how far the average purity of accepted product is from spec toward 100%, averaged over the orders weighted by size. Never paid for extra quantity (the contract also ends as soon as quantity is met). Off-spec product costs $0.08/gal and spills $0.15/gal, tallied across restarts. Time is free, so slow or odd settings are safe to explore; waste is the consequence. Payout never drops below 20% of base. Start with $250. The header shows money and an estimated payout live.
- **Store between contracts** (replaces the free draft). It shows the next contract's orders (rolled when the last one is paid) and flags a new PRODUCT outlet. 6 offers priced from their stats (bigger tanks, faster/wider-range pumps, higher-conversion/higher-rated reactors without side reactions, sharper/higher-rated columns cost more), buy any you can afford, reroll for $50 (+$25 per reroll in the same store). The new reaction's reactor and its purifying column are free.
- Volumes are gallons; reactions change volume by their coefficients (no mass balance realism).
- **Flow takes time.** Pipes hold liquid in proportion to their length (plug flow) and reactors are stirred vessels (200-350 gal) that fill before overflowing, so starting up takes about 2-4 s at 1x with typical pump rates (faster pumps start quicker). The fluid front, and later any change of composition, visibly travels along the pipes; reactors show a level gauge. Listed conversion is what a reactor gives running steadily, and liquid left in pipes and reactors when the feed runs out is lost.
- **Spills show where they happen.** An open outlet that is spilling drips into a red puddle with a red SPILL GPM readout; a full tank, stopped pump or unit on a tank-less loop gets a flashing red frame, readout and reason (TANK FULL, PUMP STOPPED, LOOP NEEDS A TANK). Hovering a unit shows how much has spilled there. The header SPILL total is what the fee is charged on.
- Flow model: see ARCHITECTURE. Liquid with nowhere to go is spilled and shown as an alarm.

## Open questions

- Balance: contract sizes, pay, bonus multiplier, fees and prices are first guesses. With the high-purity column a simple plant often earns a ~+100% purity bonus at contract 1, which may be too generous; reactors (~$650-900) may be too pricey.
- Upgrade cards (boost a placed unit's conversion/pump size/column sharpness) and selling equipment back in the store are not built yet.
- Multiple products per contract, a menu, a run summary/score screen (money earned could be the score).
- Whether to add disruptions (failures, demand spikes). User said randomness is in the equipment and chemistry, so default is no.
